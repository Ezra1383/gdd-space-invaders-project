Reversal

You will beat the final boss. That isn't the twist. The twist is what you do with the body.

  • Bullet hell
  • Java · Swing
  • Endless run
  • One sitting

The pitch

A screen full of death, and a hitbox the size of a dot

Three fleets stand between you and the end of the war. You will burn through all of them. Every shooter tells you to kill the boss — Reversal lets you become it, then shows you exactly what that's worth.

Your ship is forty-eight pixels wide. The part of it that can actually be killed is twelve. Bullets tear past the wings by a hair and you live, every single time, because you chose to be there.

  • Get paid for nerve

    Skim a bullet without touching it and you graze it — points, instantly, for the near miss. Playing it safe is playing it poor. The scoreboard belongs to the pilot who flies into the pattern.

  • Two fleets, two ways to die

    The Nairan carve the arena into lethal bands and dare you to pick a lane. The Kla'ed wall it in permanently — every chunk of health you take off their flagship slams another door, until you are duelling a dreadnought inside a corridor you built yourself.

  • A run that never repeats

    No fixed level list. A live director watches how you're doing, builds each wave out of formations on a growing budget, and drops a boss gate when the tension is right. Clear the authored run and it loops — harder, every lap.

  • And then there's Nemesis

    Your ship. Your guns. In red. It remembers every boss you have already beaten and puts them back on the table one at a time as its health falls — the Nairan rays, the Kla'ed walls, and finally vertical beams as reality starts coming apart at the seams.

The turn

Kill Nemesis and the game hands you the wreckage

It does not end. Red corruption shards start dropping, and they are right there, and it made you feel small, and it is power.

  • Each shard takes something

    Your hull stains one shade further from blue toward Nemesis red, and the Ray widens — the flagship's signature beam, now yours, firing forward through everything in its lane.

  • Five shards and there is nothing left to save

    The screen tears open. You take the boss's side of the arena — the right — and you turn to face the left, where a small blue ship has just flown in. It's you. The version that started this run.

  • It is invincible

    It grinds your health bar down while you throw everything Nemesis ever had at it. You are the boss now. Somebody else is coming for you. The loop closes.

“You never learn, do you?” — the last line of the run

Loadout

Build the pilot you want

Kills drop upgrades. Nothing here makes you faster — this game is about precision, and you will want every pixel of control you have.

DropWhat it does
Weapon Up Five tiers, from a single pellet to a plasma comet that deletes what it touches. Higher tiers cost more to reach, so firepower ramps across the whole run instead of maxing out in the first biome.
Clone A ghost ship flies your wing. It shoots. It also eats one lethal hit for you. Up to two.
Shield A charge that breaks instead of you — but only after your clones are gone. Up to three.
Corruption Unlocked only after Nemesis falls. There is no way to put it back.

Play it

Six keys, no install

ArrowsMove — full 2D, you own the left third of the arena SpaceFire, held down PPause MMute ACredits, from the title screen

Requires Java 17+ · run from the project root

# Windows (PowerShell)
javac -encoding UTF-8 -d out (Get-ChildItem -Recurse src -Filter *.java | % { $_.FullName })
java -cp out gdd.Main

# macOS / Linux
find src -name "*.java" > sources.txt && javac -encoding UTF-8 -d out @sources.txt
java -cp out gdd.Main

Pure Java and Swing — no engine, no libraries. A sixty-frame update loop, a data-driven enemy roster, a runtime wave director, and sound effects synthesised in code.

Reversal — a Game Design & Development project at Assumption University of Thailand.
